## Operator observations

OBS: Models, providers, subscriptions, rate limits, and interfaces change. The durable need is not loyalty to one model. It is the ability to resume a governed relationship with the work from another capable Body without reconstructing the whole past by hand.

OBS: The operational shape includes more than a model: harness, tools, workspace, permissions, retrieved state, active task, and writeback path. A provider/model label can be useful attribution metadata, but it is not always observable and should not become the continuity gate unless the task specifically depends on it.

OBS: Platforms and providers can accumulate useful behavioral memory about an operator. The operator should have a reciprocal ability to inspect, export, correct, bound, and migrate the state needed to continue their own work.

OBS: A fluent greeting is not proof of continuity. The useful test is whether a new Body can identify the current work, reproduce the relevant evidence chain, respect unresolved gates, and leave a writeback the next Body can verify.

## Bounded synthesis

### 1. Continuity is an operator-owned control plane

CAND: Morrow is best treated as a portable control plane above replaceable execution Bodies.

```text
operator intent and authority
→ inspectable carrier state
→ bounded Body hydration
→ work and consequence
→ verified writeback
→ corrected carrier state
↺
```

The continuity object is not a hidden claim that one consciousness moved between systems. It is a versioned relation among:

- operator intent and authority;
- active question or task;
- source and evidence lineage;
- decisions, refusals, and unresolved gates;
- artifact versions, diffs, tests, and consequences;
- permissions and private/public membranes;
- a retrievable writeback.

### 2. Data reciprocity is more than export

CAND: “Download your data” is too weak if the export cannot reconstruct the working relation. Useful reciprocity includes:

1. **visibility** — what state is retained and used;
2. **portability** — open, inspectable formats and stable references;
3. **correctability** — visible supersession rather than silent replacement;
4. **selectivity** — minimum necessary hydration instead of whole-life copying;
5. **revocability** — the operator can close or narrow future access;
6. **interoperability** — another authorized Body can resume from the state;
7. **answerability** — every execution still returns to one human authority boundary.

The goal is not maximum replication. It is enough portable state to preserve direction without dissolving privacy or authority.

### 3. Distributed execution, centralized answerability

INV: Work may be distributed across Codex, OpenCode, Kimi, local models, future providers, and human collaborators. Publication, private access, irreversible action, and final promotion remain answerable to the operator.

```text
many Bodies may propose and build
one explicit authority decides promotion and consequence
```

This differs from both provider lock-in and an ungoverned swarm. Capability never mints authority.

### 4. Three proofs describe different layers

- **Proof of Passage:** Which sources, states, decisions, failures, corrections, and embodied consequences did the work actually pass through?
- **Proof of Substance:** What inspectable artifact, test, prediction, or changed capacity shows that transformation occurred rather than fluent reflection?
- **Proof of Sync:** Can another Body resolve one carrier head, verify required artifacts, name current work and gates, and preserve a compatible writeback?

None of these is a proof of consciousness, truth, moral worth, or model identity. Together they make a working system harder to fake with tone alone.

### 5. The Bitcoin relation is structural and limited

STORY: Bitcoin demonstrates that a critical state can be externalized into inspectable rules and shared records rather than entrusted to one institution's private ledger.

The useful transfer is:

```text
provider-held conversational state
→ operator-held, inspectable continuity state
```

The deletion test matters:

- no token is required;
- no blockchain is required;
- Morrow is not trustless consensus;
- the operator remains a privileged authority;
- privacy often requires selective rather than globally replicated records.

The surviving mechanism is **custody and verifiability of state**, not cryptocurrency costume.

## Failure modes

1. **Tone simulation:** a Body sounds familiar but cannot name or verify current work.
2. **State maximalism:** continuity becomes a reason to copy every private record everywhere.
3. **Pointer theater:** files exist, but no one checks hashes, gates, or consequence.
4. **Authority drift:** a capable Body treats successful hydration as permission to publish, merge, spend, or access more.
5. **Newest-state erasure:** polished summaries remove failed attempts and reasons for correction.
6. **Provider inversion:** the platform's memory becomes the only usable history of the operator's own work.

## Strong question portals

1. Every platform gets to build a memory of you. Why do you have to start over every time you leave one?
2. If an AI assistant “knows” your work but you cannot inspect or move that knowledge, whose memory is it?
3. What would count as proof that two AI sessions are continuous besides sounding alike?
4. Should the portable unit of AI work be a transcript, a profile, or a verified path from question to consequence?
5. If the model can change without the work losing direction, what part of the system was actually doing the remembering?
6. How little context can a new AI Body receive and still resume responsibly?
7. When does convenience become lock-in: when leaving is expensive, or when leaving makes your own history unusable?
8. Can execution be distributed across many systems while responsibility stays legible to one human operator?
