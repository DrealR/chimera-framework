## One-line seam

A compulsive loop may tighten not only because one object gains weight, but because competing futures become harder to perceive, trust, and reach.

## Primary candidate post

> What if addiction isn't only wanting one thing too much—but slowly losing access to every competing future?
>
> The object stays close. Every other path starts feeling farther away.
>
> At what point does desire become a tunnel?

## Short variant

> What if addiction isn't wanting one thing too much?
>
> What if it's losing access to every competing future?

## Why this entrance carries weight

- It opens with a real question rather than a diagnosis or lecture.
- It contains an answer-bearing relation: control may change through the relative reachability of alternatives.
- It lets people answer through lived experience without requiring Framework vocabulary.
- It can connect substance use, gambling, feeds, shopping, clout, work, and other loops while explicitly refusing clinical equivalence.

## Candidate mechanism

```text
pain, pressure, desire, or objective
→ one high-weight path becomes easy to repeat
→ competing paths receive less attention and rehearsal
→ the field increasingly presents the same path as the nearest future
→ exit becomes imaginable but not reachable
```

## Desired Return

- lived distinctions between desire, habit, compulsion, and addiction;
- examples where new relationships, housing, care, work, ritual, or changed cues made another future reachable;
- counterexamples where alternatives remained visible but did not change behavior;
- corrections from clinicians, people in recovery, harm-reduction workers, families, and affected communities;
- designs that increase exit capacity without shame, coercion, or disappearance.

## Counterpaths

- Addiction is medically, psychologically, socially, and materially complex; this candidate relation is not a definition or diagnostic test.
- Some people perceive alternatives clearly but cannot access them because of withdrawal, coercion, poverty, housing, trauma, disability, law, or care scarcity.
- Removing an object or dispersing a group can move the harm without increasing recovery or agency.
- Strong desire, repetition, pleasure, fandom, work, and ordinary social-media use are not automatically addiction.
- Concentrated services and communities can make recovery more reachable rather than less.
