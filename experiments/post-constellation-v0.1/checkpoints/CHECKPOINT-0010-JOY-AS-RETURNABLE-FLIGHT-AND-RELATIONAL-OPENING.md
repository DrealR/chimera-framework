## Core synthesis

### 1. The missing integration

The pieces already existed separately:

- `hi` as the minimum shared scene;
- park guitar as a witness-gradient;
- repeated behavior as a write into later automaticity;
- flight as useful when it has a return vector;
- elasticity as maneuver without capture;
- reciprocal gaze as an invitation completed or declined by another center;
- relational distance as a vector, not physical separation alone;
- guitar as embodied, non-forced field-shaping.

The new discovery joins them into one practice:

```text
CONTACT → CUE → PULSE → SOFTEN → OFFER → READ → RELEASE → LAND
```

### 2. Joy as trained retrieval

The practice does not command happiness. Repetition may lower the access cost of a genuine playful mode just as repeated guitar work lowers the retrieval cost of a chord. The state remains callable, scene-dependent, and corrigible rather than permanent.

### 3. Flight-to-contact

Peter Pan's happy thoughts supply a takeoff carrier; Gear Five supplies elasticity; the current Framework supplies weight and landing. Mature play widens the field and returns to the person, body, consequence, and task. Joy without return becomes avoidance; weight without play becomes rigidity.

### 4. Relational opening

A smile, nod, brief eye contact, or greeting may offer a low-cost answer path. It does not “lower another person's guard” as an operator action. Reciprocity forms the micro-scene; silence, ambiguity, or withdrawal completes the attempt without debt.

### 5. Rhythm before language

Broad tempo attunement may become a pre-semantic bridge. Exact imitation is excluded. The operator notices cadence, preserves natural movement and distance, lets synchrony emerge, and releases immediately when the other body does not enter.

### 6. Perspective adds a coordinate

Perspective-taking is representational flight with a landing requirement. It adds another coordinate without erasing the first person's feeling, boundary, evidence, or consequence.

## Practice and measurement

The new Framework document defines the Flight-to-Contact loop, guitar/park practice, conflict/perspective practice, state-access rings, and failure modes.

The operator-side metrics are:

- truthful state contact;
- access to one genuine degree of play;
- less rigidity without lost boundaries;
- offering rather than demanding;
- reading the actual return;
- clean release of non-response;
- landing in embodied contact;
- widened perspective without truth erasure.

“Made them smile” is explicitly rejected as the metric.
