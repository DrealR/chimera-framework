## IDENTITY

- **Subject:** Air — the gaseous mixture primarily composed of nitrogen (~78%), oxygen (~21%), argon (~0.9%), carbon dioxide (~0.04%), and trace gases, existing as a continuous medium in Earth's atmosphere.
- **Body Type:** A fluid body with no fixed shape, occupying the space between solid and liquid surfaces, governed by pressure, temperature, and composition gradients. It is a composite body formed from the interaction of individual gas molecules, electromagnetic fields, and thermodynamic constraints.
- **Scale:** Macroscopic (observable at human scale) and microscopic (molecular collisions, mean free path ~68 nm at sea level). Operates as a continuum in fluid dynamics but as a discrete particle system in kinetic theory.
- **Lifespan:** Persists as long as Earth's gravitational field and solar energy maintain atmospheric conditions. Individual molecules cycle through biological and geological processes (e.g., oxygen in respiration, nitrogen in the nitrogen cycle), but the body as a whole is effectively perpetual under current planetary conditions.
- **Formative Sequence (Layer 2):**
  1. **~4.5 billion years ago (BYA):** Earth's formation — volatile gases (H₂O, CO₂, N₂) trapped in the planet's interior outgas during planetary differentiation, forming the primordial atmosphere (I>O: extraction from Earth's interior).
  2. **~3.8 BYA:** Emergence of life — anaerobic organisms begin modifying the atmosphere, producing methane and hydrogen sulfide (O>I: biological contribution to substrate).
  3. **~2.4 BYA:** Great Oxygenation Event — cyanobacteria produce oxygen as a metabolic byproduct, radically altering atmospheric composition (O>I: oxygen accumulates as a structural byproduct of life).
  4. **~500 million years ago (MYA):** Stabilization of modern atmospheric ratios — oxygen levels rise to ~21%, enabling aerobic respiration and the Cambrian explosion (O>I: substrate now supports complex life).
  5. **~10,000 years ago:** Human agriculture and industrialization — CO₂ and methane levels begin rising due to anthropogenic activity (I>O: extraction from geological reservoirs and biological cycles).

## BUMP DETECTION

1. **Pressure blockages:** Mountains and urban structures disrupt airflow, creating turbulence and stagnation zones (e.g., smog in valleys, heat islands in cities). The bump is **topographic** — air cannot flow through solid barriers, forcing detours that alter local climate.
2. **Chemical traps:** Pollutants (e.g., CFCs, heavy metals) accumulate in the atmosphere because they lack natural sinks. The bump is **biogeochemical** — air cannot "digest" synthetic compounds, leading to persistent scars.
3. **Thermal inversion layers:** Warm air traps cooler air near the surface, preventing vertical mixing (e.g., smog in Los Angeles). The bump is **stratification** — temperature gradients override pressure gradients, halting flow.
4. **Gravitational confinement:** Earth's gravity prevents air from escaping into space, but this also traps heat and pollutants. The bump is **planetary** — air is bound to Earth, limiting its ability to "exhale" excess energy or matter.
5. **Human extraction bottlenecks:** Fossil fuel combustion and deforestation create **unidirectional flow** — carbon moves from geological reservoirs to the atmosphere without a compensatory return pathway. The bump is **structural** — the substrate (Earth's crust) is being depleted faster than it can regenerate.
