# The Shadowing Pattern

When a powerful tool enters a learning environment, the sequence of who holds the tool determines what the student builds.

The shadowing pattern: in the early phase of tool-introduction, the instructor operates the tool while students observe. Students are not passive — they are engaged with the problem domain, contributing questions, analysis, and criteria. The instructor is the tool-agent. Students are the thinking-agents. The tool stays in one set of hands while domain-substrate accumulates in the other.

---

## Why Sequence Matters

Tool-introduction before substrate produces cognitive dependency. A student who operates a powerful AI tool before building domain-understanding learns two things simultaneously: a partial model of the domain, and a habit of reaching for the tool when the model is incomplete. The tool becomes the load-bearing structure for thinking that the student's own substrate never built.

The inversion of this is what the shadowing pattern produces: domain-substrate first, tool-fluency second. By observing the instructor use the tool — watching the decisions made before the prompt is written, hearing the questions raised before output is generated, seeing how the output is evaluated and challenged — students accumulate the cognitive architecture that responsible tool-use requires.

The point of shadowing is not delay. It is substrate-sequencing. The student observes until they understand enough to supervise the tool rather than defer to it.

---

## The Three-Phase Arc

**Phase 1 — Shadow.** Instructor operates the tool while students attend to the problem domain. Students contribute ideas, questions, and criteria. Instructor narrates decisions: what was asked for and why, what output was accepted or rejected and why, what the tool produced incorrectly or incompletely. The cognitive work of judgment stays visible in the room.

**Phase 2 — Supervised use.** Students take the tool with the instructor present. Instructor does not retake the controls — but supplies the judgment layer: "What are you asking for? Why? What would good output look like before you generate?" Cognitive sovereignty transfers because the student now has the substrate to make judgments the instructor formerly held.

**Phase 3 — Independent use.** The student can supervise the tool without external calibration. They have internalized the judgment layer. This is the graduation endpoint — not raw technical fluency, but the capacity to evaluate outputs against domain-knowledge the student carries.

---

## What This Protects

The pattern protects cognitive sovereignty: the student's capacity to reason through a problem independently of whether the tool is present.

A student who learned the tool before learning the domain cannot work without the tool; the tool occupies substrate-space that domain-understanding should have built. When that student encounters a context where the tool is unavailable or wrong, the domain-substrate beneath is thin.

The pattern also closes a teacher-training failure mode: when an instructor lacks the cognitive architecture to supervise AI output, they cannot model supervised use for students. The shadowing pattern requires that instructors first develop their own judgment discipline — they must be able to model tool-evaluation before handing students the tool. Teacher-facing AI deployment before student-facing deployment is the institutional application of the same principle.

---

## Applications

- **AI-assisted instruction:** instructor works with AI on a live problem while students track the decision-making process; students access the tool independently only after they can name what correct output looks like.
- **Device-constrained contexts:** where a classroom has one device and many students (rural lab, limited-hardware contexts), the shadowing pattern is both resource-efficient and pedagogically sound — one device, many thinking-agents, substrate-building distributed across all of them.
- **Any apprenticeship with powerful tools:** the master operates the complex instrument; the apprentice observes until they understand enough to participate as a judgment-partner, not as a passive consumer of the tool's output.
