# The Shared Pulse Protocol

> **Classification:** current group-learning and facilitation practice. Music is both an embodied implementation and a carrier for other relational bodies. The protocol is a pilot design, not a validated assessment or therapeutic intervention.

## Purpose

> **First synchronize enough to become a body. Then differentiate enough for the body to become alive.**

A shared pulse lowers uncertainty and gives many distinct bodies a common time reference. The protocol uses one accessible rhythm, build, movement, or repeated action to make coordination, listening, support, leadership, variation, and recovery observable through practice.

The song does not only train the body. It scans how the body currently relates.

## The minimum playable pattern

Do not begin with the easiest complete song. Begin with the smallest unit through which anyone present can enter:

```text
one pulse:   down — down — down — down
one chord:   Em
one phrase:  “We are here.”
one motion:  step — together — step — together
one build:   one action that works for one real user
```

Participation remains layered:

1. **Presence:** listen, observe, sway, or remain nearby.
2. **Pulse:** clap, tap, count, or mark the common time.
3. **Foundation:** repeat one note, chord, movement, role, or task.
4. **Structure:** follow the complete progression or workflow.
5. **Variation:** change expression while preserving contact.
6. **Improvisation:** introduce something new that the body can metabolize.
7. **Guidance:** help another participant enter without taking over.

> **The floor gives everyone an entrance. The open ceiling gives everyone a future.**

Presence must remain a legitimate layer. No participant should be shamed into visible performance.

## Local anchors

Distribute experienced participants throughout the group rather than concentrating them at the center. Each local anchor should be:

- close enough to perceive local difficulty;
- visible and audible enough to follow;
- responsive enough to adjust translation;
- restrained enough not to drown out the learner;
- capable of returning information to the larger group.

```text
global direction
→ section anchors
→ local groups
→ individual participants
→ local return
→ whole-body adjustment
```

The Moon is a carrier for this role: nearby, rhythmic, reflective, and relational rather than overwhelmingly source-like. In public facilitation language, **local anchor** is clearer than assigning people celestial status.

## The protocol

### 1. Name the body

Who is included, why are they together, and what are they trying to give or create?

### 2. Establish the floor

Choose one meaningful action everyone can access through at least one modality.

### 3. Find common time

Do not add complexity until the group can perceive and recover the shared rhythm.

### 4. Map present positions

Use activity evidence and the [Relational Position Map](relational-position-map-and-band-prime.md), not personality labels.

### 5. Form sections

Distribute experience, translation capacity, equipment, and patient support across local groups.

### 6. Rotate the center

Different participants lead tempo, entry, explanation, testing, dynamics, call-and-response, or reflection.

### 7. Open variation windows

Invite bounded personal expression while the common floor remains audible.

### 8. Introduce a safe disturbance

Stop the primary leader, lower one section, alter a cue, switch a role, or introduce a small error. Observe whether the group notices, communicates, redistributes, and recovers.

### 9. Return to the shared phrase

The body demonstrates recovery by finding recognizable relation again, not by pretending the disturbance never occurred.

### 10. Reflect and transfer

Ask:

- When did you feel inside the group?
- When did you feel lost or crowded?
- Who or what helped you return?
- When did difference improve the result?
- When did expression break contact?
- Did you listen while contributing?
- What should change in the arrangement next time?

Transfer the map into a school project, family routine, sports team, coding group, workplace, or community build only after naming what differs in the new domain.

## Music as distributed prediction

A song provides tempo, meter, progression, sections, and entry cues without determining the exact performance. Each participant repeatedly estimates:

```text
expected next move
→ expression
→ group response
→ correction
→ next expression
```

The score constrains a recognizable attractor. Listening and recovery let independent players create the particular performance one beat at a time.

This makes music useful for teaching relational complexity, but it does not prove that every family, market, or institution literally behaves like music.

## SPARK integration

SPARK's public method currently names seven repeating moves: Identify, Research, Connect, Brainstorm, Build, Give It, and Improve. It describes the moves as the curriculum and each real build as the practice body. [Spark Builders: The Builder's Brain](https://sparkbuilders.org/)

The Shared Pulse Protocol adds a group layer underneath any expedition:

```text
SPARK
one participant notices, investigates, builds, gives, and improves

RELATIONAL POSITION MAP
the group identifies capacities, relations, and a mission-relevant seam

SHARED PULSE
the group practices entry, coordination, difference, and recovery

REAL RECIPIENT
the result leaves the room and receives consequence
```

This is an application proposal, not evidence that every SPARK cohort or outside partner has adopted the Band Prime.

```text
4. GROUP MAP

What can every participant meaningfully do?

6. SMALLEST BUILD
What can be tested today?

7. AI ROLE
What should the machine expand, translate, visualize, or construct?

8. HUMAN ROLE
What observation, judgment, consent, and responsibility remain ours?

9. GIVE IT
Who uses it in a real setting?

10. PROOF AND RETURN
What changed, what failed, and what should the group strengthen next?
```

## AI role

Use AI only after the human body, mission, consent, and gap are named. AI may:

- generate drills at several entry levels;
- translate or reformat instructions;
- suggest role arrangements;
- create starter code or accessible representations;
- visualize a facilitator-approved group map;
- organize reflection without deleting minority observations.

AI must not:

- assign a child's value or permanent ability;
- publicly rank participants;
- infer diagnosis, emotion, or home life;
- replace facilitator observation and safeguarding;
- optimize synchronization by removing veto or difference;
- retain child data beyond the declared purpose and period.

## Facilitator safeguards

- Make participation voluntary and provide nonperforming roles.
- Design for mobility, sensory, language, and communication differences before the session.
- Use safe disturbances, never humiliation or engineered social failure.
- Watch whether expert helpers are creating capacity or dependence.
- Rotate visible leadership without forcing exposure.
- Keep the recipient and mission real; do not make children perform generosity for adult branding.
- End with rest, reflection, data deletion or retention choices, and a clear return path.

## Carry line

> **Name the body. Find its pulse. Give everyone a playable entrance. Distribute anchors. Open room for variation. Test recovery without humiliation. Then give the resulting song or build to somebody real.**
