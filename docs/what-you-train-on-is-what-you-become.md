## 0. The claim, stated once

**Humans are a continuous-learning layer — we train, every day, on what we're exposed to. AI didn't invent continuous-retraining-on-the-now; it industrialized what humans always were. So the same rule governs both: train on the raw flood and you degrade (model-collapse for the model, brain-rot for the person); curate your training data and you become yourself. Authoring your slice is choosing what shapes you.**

---

## 1. Exposure is a one-way door

Once you see the trick (the meta, the answer, the optimal route) you **can't unsee it**, and you'd be foolish not to use it — so every solution you see **permanently** changes how you'd perform. You only get to solve a given puzzle **fresh once**, and the flood spends that for you before you arrive. **Keeper:** what you expose yourself to is a *permanent* choice — which is the deepest reason to protect the blind-discovery window (`protect-the-discovery-window-explore-first`). *(It also means the flood contaminates the measure of intelligence itself: a figure-it-out-fresh test can't distinguish the one who solved it from the one who saw it online.)*

## 2. Humans are the original continuous-learner

We see, absorb, adapt — the trick goes in and changes us. The AI-mirror isn't just all-the-models retraining on the now; it's **all-the-humans-and-all-the-models.** An LLM retraining on the internet is the human mechanism at machine scale. **Keeper:** AI is a mirror because it shows us what we are — continuous learners shaped by our inputs.

## 3. The same rule governs both

Train a model on the **raw flooded internet** (including AI-slop) and it degrades — **model-collapse.** Train a person on the **raw flood** and they become a derivative, consensus-fed echo — **brain-rot.** Same problem. The model's lesson (don't train on the raw flood; curate quality data) is the **person's** lesson (don't ingest the raw flood; author your slice). **Keeper:** curating exposure = curating your own training data, and it determines who you become — the authored-slice (`the-lens-as-slice`) is training-data curation for a self.

## 4. SPARK

SPARK is **human-training-data curation in the years that shape a kid most.** The journal, by-hand making, kept friction, limited screen are **quality training-inputs** (real making, real noticing, real struggle) instead of the raw flood (the meta, the consensus, the slop). You don't train a kid on the flood; you train them on the inputs that grow an **original operator**, not a consensus-echo. The model-collapse problem and the brain-rot problem are the same problem — and curated-training is the same answer.
