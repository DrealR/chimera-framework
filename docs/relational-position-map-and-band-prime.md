# The Relational Position Map and Band Prime

> **Classification:** current group-body mapping instrument and measurement prototype. **Band Prime** is the memorable carrier for the complete group configuration. Position vectors, relationship fields, and gap priorities are operational models, not validated psychometrics or measures of human worth.

## Terminology firewall

Earlier CHIMERA material used “prime” in several ways:

- **prime body:** a smallest functionally irreducible body at a named scale;
- **relational prime:** a developmental term for an emergent composite body;
- **prime:** in these notes, a member's coordinate across selected dimensions.

These meanings should not be silently combined. Current public usage is:

| Term | Current use |
|------|-------------|
| **Member position vector** `p_i(t)` | One participant's temporary coordinate in this body, mission, and period. |
| **Group position matrix** `P_t` | The collection of member vectors. |
| **Relationship field** `R_t` | How participants enable, suppress, teach, burden, stabilize, or redirect one another. |
| **Band Prime** | Carrier name for the complete time-bound group map, not a mathematical prime or identity score. |
| **Emergent composite body** | Current replacement for the older ambiguous “relational prime” construction. |

> **A coordinate locates a body in an activity. It does not define the body's essence or rank.**

## Describe the body before measuring it

“People playing guitar” is not a sufficient body description. A usable description names:

- **membrane:** who and what are included;
- **medium:** how members exchange signal and support;
- **clock:** the rhythm and duration organizing interaction;
- **mission:** what the group is trying to create or change;
- **governance:** how tempo, roles, resources, and decisions are selected;
- **entry floor:** the smallest action through which someone can participate;
- **exit and rest:** how members pause, refuse, leave, and recover;
- **evidence horizon:** when the map expires and must be renewed.

Example:

> Twelve people meet every Sunday to learn one shared song, help new players enter the rhythm, and gradually create an original arrangement. Leadership rotates, participation is voluntary, and each session ends with reflection and rest.

## The complete body map

```text
B_t = (D, P_t, R_t, H_≤t)
```

Where:

- `D` is the body description, membrane, mission, medium, clock, and governance;
- `P_t` is the present group position matrix;
- `R_t` is the relationship field among members and dimensions;
- `H_≤t` is the relevant history and evidence that produced the present configuration.

A body is not only its member list or average score. It is members plus relation, arrangement, history, and direction.

## Member position vectors

The group selects only dimensions required by its current mission. A band might use:

```text
p_i(t) = [pulse, listening, entry, dynamics,
          improvisation, recovery, confidence]
```

Another mission requires another vector. A classroom accessibility project may need interviewing, research, explanation, prototyping, testing, documentation, and care.

Each coordinate should include:

- a bounded scale with behavioral anchors;
- the participant's self-location;
- evidence or an example;
- optional peer or facilitator observation;
- uncertainty or “not yet observed”;
- a date and activity context.

Say:

> “Your current evidence places you around level 2 for entry timing in this arrangement.”

Never:

> “You are a 2.”

## Relationship field

Individual scores cannot explain whether a group can perform. Two experts may compete for the center, ignore beginners, or destabilize timing. A less technical member may preserve the whole body through listening, translation, encouragement, or recovery.

For participants `i` and `j` along dimension `d`, use a qualitative or carefully bounded relation:

```text
R_ij(d) = how i changes j's available capacity along d
```

Questions include:

- Who makes another member more confident or more hesitant?
- Who translates the global instruction into a locally playable move?
- Who takes over rather than teaching?
- Who restores the group after error?
- Who introduces variation without breaking contact?
- Where does one person's strength conceal group dependency?

> **The central unit is not “How good is each person?” It is “What becomes possible when these people enter relation in this arrangement?”**

## Gap priority

A simple spread is:

```text
G_d = max position on d − min position on d
```

But the widest spread may be harmless. A first-pass priority heuristic is:

```text
Priority_d = Gap_d × MissionImportance_d × ConnectionImpact_d
```

This is an operator instrument, not scientific multiplication. Each term requires evidence and explanation. A small listening gap can matter more than a large improvisation gap if it repeatedly breaks the shared pulse.

Ask:

> **Which distance is preventing this body from remaining connected while moving toward its present mission?**

Then inspect whether the cause is:

- missing practice;
- unclear or inaccessible instruction;
- equipment or resource limits;
- fear or social risk;
- sensory or language mismatch;
- one dominant member taking too much space;
- wrong role or instrument;
- a poorly chosen dimension;
- a badly designed environment.

Sometimes the intervention belongs in the participant. Sometimes it belongs in the arrangement.

## Raise the floor; preserve the solo

The Band Prime holds two simultaneous commitments.

### Raise the floor

Every member receives a viable entrance through simplification, translation, pacing, equipment, pairing, demonstration, or another role.

### Preserve the solo

Once common timing exists, members may remix, improvise, change instrument, tell the story, document, cook, design, dance, or contribute through another capacity.

> **The floor creates belonging. The solo creates emergence.**

Maximum sameness is not the objective. Maximum synchronization is not health: armies, coercive groups, and outrage systems can synchronize bodies while destroying veto and differentiation.

Living coherence requires at least:

- sufficient synchronization for contact;
- access to the shared floor;
- differentiated expression;
- voluntary participation and veto;
- recovery after disturbance;
- circulation of leadership;
- an external mission or recipient beyond group self-display.

## Data and child-safety rules

- Use the smallest set of dimensions needed for the mission.
- Prefer evidence from activity over personality labels.
- Do not create public leaderboards or permanent deficit profiles.
- Give participants access to, correction of, and meaningful deletion of their map.
- Separate facilitator observations from facts and record disagreement.
- Do not infer disability, diagnosis, emotion, or family condition from performance.
- Design accessible entry routes before interpreting low participation as low capacity.
- Expire coordinates; do not carry a score into another body without fresh evidence and consent.
- AI may help visualize or generate bridges but must not assign worth, discipline children, or make consequential rankings.

## Firewalls

- A vector is a map chosen by operators, not the person.
- Quantification can conceal cultural, accessibility, power, and role-design assumptions.
- Range and averages lose relationship structure.
- Peer assessment can reproduce popularity and bias.
- A group's mission may itself be wrong or coercive.
- “Close the gap” must never mean normalize every difference.
- Do not use prime language to imply mathematical proof, indivisibility, destiny, or innate hierarchy.

## Carry line

> **Describe the body. Map present positions without turning them into identities. Read what happens between positions. Raise the floor that permits entry, preserve the differences that create emergence, and change the arrangement before blaming the person.**
