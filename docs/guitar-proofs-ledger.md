# Guitar Proofs Ledger

Earned items from the guitar walk — kept because the proof is the fingering, not the map, and because each one ported a general rule out of a specific rep.

## The G-to-B seam

Standard tuning stacks the six strings in perfect fourths — *except one*. E→A, A→D, D→G, B→E are all perfect fourths (5 semitones). G→B is a **major third** (4 semitones). One deliberate flaw in an otherwise regular grid.

It's not a mistake; it's a trade-off chosen on purpose. The major-third gap at G-B is what makes open chords and common shapes physically reachable — a fully regular all-fourths tuning would put familiar chord shapes out of a hand's span. So the grid accepts one asymmetry to buy playability everywhere else. And the cost is real and constant: **every shape warps when it crosses the G-B boundary** — the chord that's a clean geometric form on the lower strings needs a one-fret shift the moment it spans that pair.

**The generalized rule** (this is why it's in the framework, not just the music section): *hunt any domain's seam* — the single asymmetry that everything else negotiates around. It's usually a built-in trade-off, it's usually where the design paid for its strengths, and it's usually where the real understanding of the domain lives. A learner who finds the seam stops memorizing exceptions and starts seeing the one decision that generates them. The seam is also the second leg of the [aliveness test](the-seed-protocol.md): every real instrument has its G-to-B; a body with no seam faced no trade-off and is probably hollow.

## Correction logged: the open strings are not "one complete E"

A tempting overclaim, killed: the open strings E-A-D-G-B-E are *not* "one big E chord" just because both ends are E. Stacked, the open strings spell an **Em11** (E-A-D-G-B-E = root, 11th, ♭7, ♭3, 5, octave). The two E bookends are real and worth noting; the middle four are genuinely other notes, not decoration on an E. Logged here as a specimen of the literal jump caught in the framework's own backyard: "both ends are E, so it's an E" is a structural rhyme promoted past what the notes support.
