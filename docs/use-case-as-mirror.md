# Use Case as Mirror

> Every use case is a reflection of the same underlying structure. The variation is in the mirror — the geography, the domain vocabulary, the community — not in the problem shape itself.

---

## Core Claim

What looks like separate problems across separate contexts is usually **one problem showing up in multiple mirrors.**

Brooklyn kids without laptops and Tumaini students without laptops are the same gap structure, different geography. Chess teaching systems-thinking and community problems teaching systems-thinking are the same transfer principle, different domain. Youth-builder methodology and STEM fellowship pedagogy are the same instructional primitive, different occasion.

The mirror is the use case. It gives the underlying primitive its local shape — cultural vocabulary, specific constraints, community history. But the structure underneath is the same.

Recognizing this is the posture shift. Instead of treating each new context as a problem that needs to be invented from scratch, the question becomes: **where have I already encountered this structure, and what did I learn there?**

---

## The Multiplication Effect

Each new use case does something the original articulation cannot do alone: it **shows the primitive from another angle.**

A principle stated in one context is only as strong as that context's examples. The same principle stated across five contexts becomes load-bearing — visible from multiple angles, tested against multiple constraints, refined by the differences between mirrors.

The use case does not dilute the principle. It sharpens it. The more contexts that hold the same structure, the more the structure earns its generality.

---

## The Sharing Posture

The mirror framing dissolves the idea-as-property frame.

If one community's teaching methodology reflects the same structure as another community's methodology across a different geography, protecting the first articulation from the second context is incoherent. You cannot own a reflection. The structure belongs to the work, not to whoever first articulated it in one mirror.

This makes sharing free — not as a moral stance but as a direct consequence of the structure: the value of a primitive increases when more mirrors reflect it. Protecting it from other mirrors decreases its value.

**Share freely. The free circulation is what sharpens the primitive.**

---

## When Mirrors Diverge

Not every apparent similarity is a genuine reflection. The discipline:

1. **Spot the structure, not the surface.** Geography, domain, vocabulary differ by design. The structure underneath may match or may be a different structure wearing similar clothes.
2. **Check what the mirror changes.** Different use cases impose different constraints. The constraint irrelevant in one mirror may be load-bearing in another.
3. **Let the divergence teach.** Where the same structure breaks down in a new mirror, the breakdown names a real constraint that was not visible before. The failed reflection is data, not failure.

This separates genuine structural recognition from cargo-cult transfer: the divergences teach as much as the convergences.
