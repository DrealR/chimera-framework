# Relative Timescales and Nested Mortality

> **Classification:** cross-scale body and operator mechanism.

## Claim

A body may function as effectively permanent to a shorter-lived observer while remaining finite in its own history.

> **Relative permanence is the appearance of endurance produced when one body’s continuity greatly exceeds another body’s meaningful planning horizon.**

This is not literal immortality.

## Relative duration ratio

A useful qualitative ratio is:

```text
R_duration
= expected continuity of observed body
  / meaningful planning horizon of observer
```

When `R_duration` is extremely large, the observed body can operate as environmental permanence.

Examples:

- a parent may feel world-like to a young child;
- a long-lived institution may appear older and more stable than any employee;
- the Sun is functionally enduring across a human lifetime;
- a platform may outlast individual user identities;
- a rapid AI output may appear instantaneous relative to human task time.

> **A body can be functionally enduring from one observer’s timescale while remaining mortal at another scale.**

## Nested mortality

Bodies end at different rates.

```text
cell
inside organism
inside relationship or institution
inside civilization
inside planetary and stellar systems
```

The nesting does not guarantee that larger equals longer-lived. Persistence depends on:

- repair;
- replacement;
- metabolism;
- environment;
- memory;
- continuity carriers;
- boundary stability;
- disturbance rate.

The useful question is not “Which body is immortal?” but:

> **Which body’s timescale is governing the current relation?**

## Temporal power

A body that persists longer, remembers more cycles, or acts much faster can repeatedly set conditions for another body.

```text
long-lived or fast body
-> establishes environment
-> shorter-lived or slower body adapts
-> asymmetry compounds
```

This creates temporal power even without direct command.

Examples:

- parent and child;
- institution and employee;
- landlord and temporary tenant;
- state and citizen;
- platform and user;
- AI system and human decision-maker;
- present generation and future generation.

> **The body that persists longer or acts faster carries a greater obligation not to treat the shorter body as disposable.**

Duration does not create moral superiority. It increases capacity to shape conditions and survive delayed consequences.

## Apparent instantaneity

A process can be real and still appear instantaneous from another frame.

```text
R_latency
= process duration / operator planning horizon
```

When `R_latency` approaches zero from the operator’s perspective, the process feels teleported.

AI is a strong example:

- output may arrive in seconds;
- computation still consumed time and energy;
- training compressed prior human passage;
- local understanding may not have formed;
- consequence still settles in slower human and institutional bodies.

Fast generation increases the need for slower verification and digestion.

## Non-identical return

A local cycle may repeat while the larger body moves.

```text
local return
+ moving containing body
+ accumulated residue
= non-identical return
```

This applies to:

- orbital systems;
- habits;
- musical phrases;
- seasons;
- family rituals;
- political cycles;
- learning loops.

> **A cycle can return to the same local phase without returning to the same total state.**

## Cross-scale care

A healthy relation across unequal timescales preserves the shorter body’s reality.

Ask:

1. Which body acts faster?
2. Which body persists longer?
3. Who controls memory and the environment?
4. Who can wait out the other?
5. Who experiences the consequences first?
6. Can the shorter body refuse, revise, or exit?
7. Is the longer body preserving local agency or treating it as replaceable?
8. What continuity should survive after the shorter body ends?
9. What must the longer body forget, release, or retire?
10. Does speed outrun verification or consent?

## Firewalls

- Larger bodies do not universally live longer.
- Long life does not imply wisdom.
- Short life does not imply lesser value.
- Faster output does not imply no work occurred.
- Mortality is not automatically beneficial, and preservation is not automatically pathological.
- This model does not establish biological lifespan from physical size.
- Orbital “spirals” depend on coordinate frame; the portable mechanism is nested motion and non-identical return.
