## 1. Two locked loops; the inverse of the panopticon

Looking at a rock is one-way. The eye-lock is two-way and **recursive** — each models the other modeling them, and the loops lock; that recursion is the intimacy (two minds aware of each other *as minds*). **Keeper:** the panopticon is seeing-*without*-being-seen (one-way, write-access taken without mutuality — the harm); the eye-lock is its inverse (mutual, consensual, both holding each other's attention). Mutuality is the whole difference between violation and intimacy.

## 2. The capacity to return the gaze = the capacity for WE = 1

**Keeper:** the looked-back-at capacity isn't intelligence — it's the capacity to be the **second pole** (recognition/mutuality, not problem-solving; cats/dogs/humans have it, insects likely don't). And that *is* the capacity for the combined-body: the eye-lock is the **smallest unit of the shared body forming** (`pair-physics`, `we-equals-one-formalization`) — the first spark before any shared context. *(Consciousness tie, held carefully: returning the gaze requires an interior, a someone-home; the eye-lock is two interiors recognizing each other. ## 3. Recognition is partly constitutive of the self

**Keeper:** you don't become a full self in isolation — the infant becomes a someone partly through being *seen* (the mother's gaze). Being seen by another interior is partly what makes and *holds* you an interior — which is why being-seen-back matters so much (why solitary confinement is torture, why the unseen feel like they're disappearing). Recognition is load-bearing, not decoration.

## 4. The flood, automation, and the harm-restraint

The internet maximizes **one-way looking** and starves the mutual gaze — you see everyone and lock with no one (the visibility-reweighting without the connection), which is why it can feel isolating despite all the seeing; the eye-lock only happens in **mutual presence.** **Keeper (the dark one):** automation removes mutual gaze at scale (apps over clerks, drones over soldiers) — and mutual gaze *restrains violence* (you have to look at the person; the eye-lock makes them a someone), so killing at a distance is easier precisely because it removes the eye-lock. Removing mutual gaze removes the recognition that holds us human to each other.

## 5. SPARK

SPARK is, at base, putting kids back in **rooms where they're seen and learn to see back** — choosing the two-way gaze the automated world engineers out. Being seen back is partly what makes a self; choosing rooms where the gaze goes both ways is choosing to stay human, and to keep others human to you.
