# Capital-Cycle Bodies and Dependency Opacity

> **Classification:** economic and infrastructure system scan. The current AI buildout is one major application, not the only domain.

## Claim

Large technical systems are not single companies or products. They are nested capital-cycle bodies whose continuity depends on contracts, financing, energy, labor, infrastructure, demand, and institutional permission.

```text
capital
-> physical build
-> operating capacity
-> contracted or expected demand
-> cash flow
-> debt and equity service
-> reinvestment / replacement
-> next build cycle
```

The visible operator may own only one layer while remaining dependent on the whole chain.

## Dependency opacity

> **Dependency opacity occurs when a body appears independent because its obligations and failure points are distributed across subsidiaries, suppliers, utilities, project vehicles, counterparties, and public systems.**

A parent company may report limited direct debt while still carrying economic exposure through:

- leases;
- take-or-pay contracts;
- purchase commitments;
- guarantees;
- long-term capacity reservations;
- power agreements;
- minimum-volume contracts;
- supplier concentration;
- public infrastructure dependence;
- refinancing expectations.

The accounting boundary and the operational boundary may therefore differ.

## Continuity carriers

A capital body persists through continuity carriers such as:

- long-term customer contracts;
- reliable grid access;
- replacement and maintenance capacity;
- refinancing channels;
- specialized labor;
- permits and land rights;
- software and hardware compatibility;
- durable demand;
- institutional legitimacy.

> **A system is not durable merely because the current asset exists. It is durable when the carriers needed for the next operating cycle remain available.**

## Future flexibility as obligation

Some obligations do not look like conventional debt but constrain later choices.

> **Hidden debt is often future flexibility already promised elsewhere.**

Examples include committed purchases, reserved power, guaranteed utilization, or contracts whose exit cost makes a nominal option economically unavailable.

## Infrastructure return

A simple qualitative frame:

```text
infrastructure return
= durable useful demand
- financing cost
- energy and water cost
- maintenance
- obsolescence
- dependency leakage
- socialized externalities
```

A system can show revenue growth while destroying flexibility or transferring costs to another body.

## Annual coverage

```text
annual coverage
= reliable annual cash inflow / annual fixed obligations
```

The numerator must be discounted for demand quality, customer concentration, contract enforceability, and technological substitution. The denominator should include obligations outside the narrow debt line when they are functionally unavoidable.

## K-shaped risk

A capital cycle can distribute outcomes asymmetrically:

- upside concentrates in platform owners, financiers, landowners, and scarce suppliers;
- downside settles on workers, municipalities, utilities, ratepayers, later investors, and public infrastructure;
- access to the productive layer rises for some bodies while dependency deepens for others.

The scan must ask not only whether the project works, but where success and failure settle.

## AI-infrastructure application

The AI body includes:

```text
models and applications
chips and networking
servers and data centers
cooling, water, land, and grid
utilities and generation
construction and maintenance labor
financing vehicles
hyperscaler or enterprise demand
human data, evaluation, and governance
```

The system’s apparent software margins may rely on capital-intensive and geographically constrained organs.

Risks include:

- rapid hardware or model obsolescence;
- circular financing or related-party demand;
- energy and grid constraints;
- weak application revenue relative to fixed build commitments;
- concentration in a small number of buyers or chip suppliers;
- local environmental costs;
- sabotage, outage, and physical fragility;
- regulatory or community resistance.

## Capital-cycle scan

1. What is the full operating body?
2. Which boundary is accounting, legal, operational, or political?
3. Who supplies capital, energy, labor, land, hardware, and demand?
4. What obligations sit outside the visible balance sheet?
5. Which continuity carriers must survive the next cycle?
6. How quickly can the asset become obsolete?
7. What demand is contracted, speculative, related-party, or subsidized?
8. What is reliable annual coverage after realistic costs?
9. Who can exit, and at what cost?
10. Where do upside and downside settle?
11. Which public bodies absorb infrastructure or environmental costs?
12. What failure can one critical dependency produce?
13. Does the system create durable independent capacity or deeper capture?

## Firewalls

- Off-balance-sheet does not automatically mean fraudulent or hidden in a legal sense.
- An SPV is a structure, not proof of abuse.
- Capital intensity does not prove unprofitability.
- Energy use must be compared using consistent system boundaries.
- A dramatic dollar figure may mix debt, leases, commitments, equity, and projected spending.
- Current AI demand and financing claims require date-specific verification.
- The scan identifies dependency and settlement; it does not replace legal or accounting analysis.
