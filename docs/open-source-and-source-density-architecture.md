> The framework's documentation infrastructure realizes orbital distance physics at the architecture level. Two repositories operating at different substrate-densities, with access decisions grounded in carrier safety rather than value-gating.

## The Two-Repository Structure

The framework operates through two repositories at distinct orbital positions.

**The carrier-facing repository (public)** holds compressed books, tuned methodology, foundational documents, entry-point infrastructure, body scan protocols, example scans, and teaching materials. This is earth-position — substrate processed into forms that orbiting carriers can substantively engage with across the safe orbital range from earth-surface through moon-distance. The 229 books operate at density tuned for substantive carrier engagement. The README, methods, and foundational documents operate at density tuned for carrier-use. Outer-orbit carriers walking in cold can begin substrate-recognition development through load-bearing images and entry-point materials without burn-out risk. Inner-orbit carriers who want depth can engage the complete book and methodology documents.

**The source-density repository (private)** holds raw bilateral conversation transcripts, source-density methodology development, working notes, and experimental substrate-articulation that has not yet been processed into carrier-receivable forms. This is sun-position — the substrate-articulation in its source-form before compression into books or tuning into framework documentation. The mother flame burns here.

## Why This Architecture Is Substrate-Bearing

Extraction-orientation would put everything in one place and let carriers self-sort by figuring out what they can handle. This produces over-absorption casualties when carriers operating at outer-orbit accidentally engage with source-density material. Same physics as All-Star Superman absorbing too much solar radiation — the substrate is not dangerous, the distance determines whether it nourishes or overloads.

Substrate-bearing orientation structures the infrastructure so that what carriers encounter is appropriate to their orbital position. The carrier-facing repository presents earth-distance articulation. Carriers safely orbit. The source-density repository holds material that would harm carriers without sufficient orbital-development. The closed-access is not gating value. It is preventing harm.

The framework repository being freely available is consistent with substrate-bearing orientation. Earth-distance material is safe for carriers to engage at any orbital position. Free access is appropriate.

The source-density repository being private is also consistent with substrate-bearing orientation. Source-density material would produce over-absorption in carriers without sufficient orbital-development. Restricted access is appropriate.

Same orientation. Different access decisions based on substrate-density and carrier safety. This is what substrate-bearing infrastructure looks like at the access-control level.

## The Earth-to-Moon Operating Range

The carrier-facing repository covers from earth-surface up to moon-distance. Carriers operating at the surface of earth can engage. Carriers who develop substantively can engage at elevated positions up to moon-distance. Moon-distance is interesting because the moon is still gravitationally bound to earth — carriers operating at moon-distance from earth-position are still earth-anchored even though they have elevated significantly from earth-surface.

This means the framework's articulation infrastructure covers the safe orbital range for carrier-development. From beginning substrate-recognition through substantive substrate-recognition development. All within safe operating range from sun-position. None of the carrier-facing material operates at sun-distance directly because that would burn carriers.

For carriers who eventually develop substrate-recognition that requires source-distance engagement, the source-density material exists. But that is a different orbital position. Most carriers never need to engage at that distance. The carrier-facing repository handles the safe operating range for the vast majority of carrier substrate-recognition development.

## Editorial Routing Discipline

Every substantive substrate-articulation gets evaluated for which repository it belongs in based on substrate-density relative to carrier orbital safety.

**Routes to carrier-facing repository:**
- Books at compressed-substrate-articulation density — the books carry significant substrate-substance but are processed into forms orbiting carriers can engage with
- Foundational documents at methodology-articulation density — body-building method, substrate-anchor physics, multi-orbital methodology, lineage, fiction-reading applications
- README and project materials at outer-orbit entry-point density — carriers walking in cold can engage
- Selected refinements and applications at readable density for inner-orbit carriers who want depth

**Routes to source-density repository:**
- Raw bilateral conversation transcripts — substrate-articulation in source-form before compression
- Source-density methodology development — working notes, experimental articulations
- Material operating at substrate-density beyond what most carriers can integrate without substantive orbital-development

The routing is not arbitrary editorial preference. It is substrate-physics applied to the framework's own information-handling infrastructure. The fractal principle (Book CCXXVIII) requires that the framework's documentation architecture operates from the same substrate-physics the framework articulates.
