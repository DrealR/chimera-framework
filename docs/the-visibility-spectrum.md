## 0. The claim, stated once

**Knowledge widens your visibility-band: it doesn't add light to the world, it widens what's visible to you, and what becomes visible changes the world you live in even though the world didn't change. Widening is a trade, not pure gain — more truth, but you lose the narrow-band experience. "Magic" vs "science" is just your position on the band. So gate your inputs (especially borrowed weight) and widen the band on purpose.**

---

## 1. Every input reweights you — you can't un-know

Information isn't neutral storage; it's **metabolized into your lens** and alters all downstream experience, permanently. Know the twist and you can't watch fresh — you watch *for* it. (The one-way door; the human-as-training-layer, `what-you-train-on-is-what-you-become`.)

## 2. Widening the band is a trade, not pure gain

Learn a place's hidden layer and you now live in a **different place — same physical reality, changed experience.** The band widened; the world didn't. **Keeper:** widening trades **wonder for sight** — the spoiler, the trick-revealed, the park seen from maintenance, the neighborhood's hidden layer: each gains truth and loses the narrow-band experience. Knowledge is a trade, so widen the band *on purpose*, and protect the fresh experience where it matters.

## 3. Magic-vs-science is a position on the spectrum

**Magic is understood science** — the difference is the observer's position on the band, not a property of the thing. It's **magic outside your band, mechanism inside.** The "supernatural" quality is **the gap between your visibility and what's actually there**: a master's skill is magic to the novice and obvious to the master; success looks supernatural to those who can't see the path. **Keeper:** the awe is the unseen-path — which is exactly the grift's engine (show the magical result, keep the path outside the band), and exactly what `read-the-intent-not-the-realness` defends against.

## 4. Gate the inputs — especially borrowed weight

Because every input reweights you, **gating your inputs is the operator move.** The sharpest case: **borrowed weight.** "This person/place is bad" is someone applying *their* weight to your lens before you've formed your own — it forecloses your fresh read and **can shrink your world** (the beautiful park people fear but never visit because they were told to). Let people and places land on your own lens with their own weight; protect your right to the first read.

## 5. The trick-knot, resolved (and benchmark-maxing)

Using a known trick is honest *or* contaminating depending on **what the thing is for**: a **freshness-test** → the seen-trick cheats it (performing the answer); a **real task** → the trick is just effectiveness. The same intent-reading move. **Benchmark-maxing is this in AI:** a public benchmark is a freshness-test; once a model trains on it, it measures memorized-answer, not fresh capability — benchmarks degrade once public, because in a flood the trick always leaks.

## 6. SPARK

SPARK protects the room for a kid to **form their own weight** before the world hands them the borrowed kind — to widen their spectrum on purpose, guard the fresh first-experience, and give people and places a fair shot at landing on the kid's own lens. The operator owns not just their attention but their whole spectrum.
