# Belief and the Three Probabilities

> **Classification:** probability-field firewall and behavioral mechanism. The taxonomy preserves belief's real causal force without turning conviction into a wish machine.

## 1. Epistemic probability

Belief changes an operator's estimate:

> How likely do I think this outcome is?

New evidence, testimony, models, and market prices can update this probability. The world has not necessarily changed; the estimate has.

## 2. Perceptual probability

Belief changes what is likely to become foreground and how an ambiguous signal is interpreted:

> What am I prepared to notice or call evidence?

Repeated weighting alters salience, search behavior, recall, and framing. This can reveal neglected structure or create confirmation loops.

## 3. Causal probability

Belief changes action, and action changes what can actually occur:

> What becomes more or less likely because bodies organize around this belief?

Preparation, investment, avoidance, coordination, practice, and institutional policy can move the outcome. The effect is causal through behavior, not a wish acting on matter without a path.

## The loop

```text
signal
→ interpretation
→ belief
→ attention and preparation
→ action
→ changed field
→ consequence
→ revised belief
```

Social media circulates and illuminates belief. AI branches and reorganizes possible belief. Markets make some beliefs numerically and financially legible. Bodies decide whether the weighting becomes action. Earth settles the result.

## Probability scan

1. Did only my estimate change?
2. Did my attention begin selecting different evidence?
3. Did any body change behavior?
4. Is there a causal path from that behavior to the claimed outcome?
5. Which independent evidence could reverse the belief?
6. Who benefits if the three probabilities are collapsed?

## The disciplined formulation

> **Attention does not select a magical universe. It selects which possibilities receive rehearsal, labor, resources, and coordination.**

Belief can be powerful because bodies act, not because conviction certifies ontology.

## Firewalls

- Felt certainty is not epistemic probability.
- Visibility is not prevalence.
- Market price is not truth.
- A self-fulfilling effect does not prove the original belief was independently accurate.
- A belief can be causally powerful and morally destructive.
