# Energy: The Cost of Crossing

*A framework study. Energy is what it costs to direct attention — and especially to cross (change direction). Every installed weight deepens a channel, so redirection gets more expensive over a life: the cross-as-threshold with a price tag that compounds. Two operator moves fall out: curate young (early channels are deepest), and keep paying the cost of crossing (don't ossify). Graduated from a core capture. Internal register.*

## 0. The claim, stated once

**Attention is a fluid, but moving it — especially redirecting it against its established channel — costs energy, and channels deepen with use, so redirection gets more expensive the longer you've flowed one way. Crossing (changing direction) is the cross-as-threshold with a price tag, and the price rises with how worn the old path is. Every weight you install deepens a channel, so the accumulated installs are the rising cost of redirection — which is why "getting stuck in your ways" is partly a flood-and-install problem, not just aging.**

---

## 1. Energy is the cost of directing — and crossing

Putting attention somewhere takes energy; **course-correcting takes more**; and the longer you've gone one way, the more it takes to change. **Keeper:** the channels deepen with use, so redirection gets more expensive over time — the elder's low battery is partly the accumulated cost of all the deepened channels, not just tiredness.

## 2. The cross with a price tag

Crossing is the cross-as-threshold (`the-cross-as-the-operators-shape`): the death of the old direction for the new, with an **energy cost that rises with how deeply the old path is worn.** The caterpillar→butterfly is a *total dissolution* — transformation is expensive because you dissolve the deeply-built old structure; the more built, the more it costs. **Young = shallow channels** (cheap to cross, little built yet); **old = deep channels** (expensive to cross, rich structure).

## 3. Every install deepens a channel

**Keeper:** taking in a belief/judgment/path/opinion isn't just carrying it — you wear a channel your attention then flows down by default, and the more you've installed (`knowing-as-installation`), the more channels you must cross out of to change. Getting-stuck is the weight of everything you accepted without choosing — a self running in deep grooves it mostly didn't choose.

## 4. Curate young — the strongest reason

**The channels you wear early are the deepest and most expensive to cross later.** Early inputs don't just shape you — they wear the *deepest grooves*. **Keeper:** the strongest argument yet for curating a kid's training data (`what-you-train-on-is-what-you-become`) — install good channels early (real making, noticing, own walked weights) and the deep grooves are good; install the flood early (consensus, harvest, borrowed weights) and the deep grooves are a derivative self that's expensive to escape. The kid's channels are shallow and cheap to redirect *now*, and only get more expensive — so now is when it matters most.

## 5. The operator keeps paying the cost (don't ossify)

**Keeper:** you can choose to spend energy on crossing while you still have it — staying flexible into age isn't luck, it's whether you kept *paying the cost of redirection* (questioning your channels, crossing, the do-your-own-walk). The operator deliberately spends energy on course-correction so the channels never fully ossify; cheaper when young, a discipline to keep as energy declines. SPARK: wear good channels early, and keep the muscle of redirection alive — cross while it's cheap, and keep crossing on purpose.
