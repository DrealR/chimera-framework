## 1. One skill, three faces

- **Read the intent, not the realness** — on a post (what is this *doing*?).
- **Judge the action, not the essence** — on a person (`judge-actions-not-essences`).
- **Strip the meat, find the bones** — on an idea.

**Keeper:** all three are the same move — look past the surface (the cosmic register, the engagement framing, the manufactured aura) to the load-bearing structure; keep what bears weight, decline the rest.

## 2. The meat is engineered to resist stripping

The hard-to-strip pieces are often the ones *most built to move you.* When an idea buries its one real bone under thick language, **the burial is the product** — see the skeleton instantly (one decent idea, sold as access) and you wouldn't subscribe. The seductive meat is load-bearing for the **funnel**, not for the **truth**. **Keeper:** stripping is the *defense* against a design built to keep you in the meat where the awe lives and the sale closes — so the harder a thing is to cut through, the more it pays to cut.

## 3. The tell: where structure becomes reach

You can do the first-pass strip yourself. The tell is **the seam where it stops being structure and starts being reach** — where the grounded part becomes the inflated part. The **"it gets hard to read" feeling is the meat thickening at the seam.** That crossing-point is the tell: the place it gets hard to read is usually the place where structure ends and reach begins.

## 4. SPARK

SPARK teaches the method, not the verdict — not *what* to think about a thing, but *how* to find the bones, how to see the structure under the surface so a kid isn't moved by the meat. The literacy that scales: read-the-intent, judge-the-action, strip-the-meat — one skill, taught early.
