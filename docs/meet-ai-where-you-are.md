# Meet AI Where You're At

The [walk/teleport criteria](walk-teleport-criteria.md) govern *which stretches* to hand to the machine. This document governs a different axis entirely: *which altitude* to operate at. They are orthogonal, and conflating them is a common, expensive mistake.

## The two regimes

**AI at your level** automates roads you've already walked. Your evaluator is intact; the license is earned; it's pure compounding. This is the photographer who can study their own work alongside the machine, replicate themselves faster, and keep their element — every output passes through an alive evaluator, so speed is gain with no loss.

**AI above your level** produces deliverables you can't read. And here the [evaluator test](walk-teleport-criteria.md) doesn't just *fail* — it **can't be administered.** You don't know that you don't know. This is the mechanism under "you don't even know what good looks like": the early builder shipping things they couldn't build isn't borrowing capability, they're operating *blind above their own ceiling and can't see the ceiling.*

## The rule

**Capability may exceed your level only on stretches whose evaluation does not.** You can let the machine execute far beyond what you could execute — *if* you can still judge the result. The honest way to reach higher is to **raise your level first** (learning mode), then let the automation follow you up. Meet it where you are, then walk upward together.

This dissolves the "two modes" observation into one knob:

- **Learning mode** = walking stretches to expand the license.
- **Shipping mode** = spending the license.
- **Failure state** = shipping perpetually on a license you never earned. Borrowed forever — the kid who teleported first and never went back to walk.

## The refinement: level is a climb rate, not an altitude

From the Theseus correction: slow plank-by-plank replacement preserves identity, but swapping four planks overnight is absurd — and that's a *refinement* of the original answer, not a footnote. The continuity carrier needs **time per plank**, not just correct sequence, because the carrier is the pattern *metabolizing* each change before the next lands. (This is the framework's pain definition again: pain is rate of change exceeding what the body can metabolize.)

So transformation has a **maximum safe speed.** Exceed it and even gradual-looking change becomes teleportation in chunks. The real variable under "meet it where you're at" is therefore the **metabolization rate** — your level isn't a fixed altitude you occupy, it's a *climb rate* you can sustain. Push the machine faster than you can metabolize what it returns and you're teleporting regardless of how incremental it looks on the surface.
