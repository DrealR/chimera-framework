# The Two Roots

Most deep problems are one of two primitives in costume.

**Root one — identity through transformation.** Does a thing survive change of its parts or state? Theseus's ship, a person across decades, a company after the founders leave, a codebase after a rewrite. The question is always: what is the *continuity carrier* — the thing tracked through time while parts get swapped?

**Root two — traversal.** How does a body get from A to B? Movement, friction, time. Learning is traversal. Building is traversal. A career is traversal. The physical anchor: a photon experiences zero proper time — traversal is only nonzero from outside frames. "Instantaneous" is always frame-relative; somebody's frame paid the time.

## The collision point

Teleportation is where the two roots meet head-on: a perfect answer to root two achieved by destroying what root one requires. The traveler arrives instantly — but the continuity carrier (the body that would have persisted through the journey) was deleted and reassembled. Traversal solved; identity detonated. The more radically a solution collapses one gap, the larger the gap it tends to open. This is a rough conservation law worth checking on any "perfect" solution.

## Why this matters now

**AI work is a teleportation event in this exact sense.** The path is walked inside the machine, in a frame the human cannot experience. The deliverable arrives at B fully formed, instantly from the human's point of view. So the identity question fires on arrival, every time: *is the thing that arrived yours?*

The human/AI ratio debate is a special case of root one. It resolves mechanically — see [the seed protocol](the-seed-protocol.md): the build is yours if and only if you supplied the continuity carrier. And the decision of which stretches to hand to the machine at all is governed by the [walk/teleport criteria](walk-teleport-criteria.md).

## Using the roots

When a problem feels deep and tangled, ask which root it is in costume. "Should I let AI write this?" is root one (will the output still be mine?) wearing a productivity costume. "Why does this feel hollow even though it works?" is root one. "Why is learning this so slow?" is root two — and the slowness may be load-bearing (see [identity as residue of walked paths](identity-as-residue-of-walked-paths.md)). Naming the root collapses most of the argument before it starts.
