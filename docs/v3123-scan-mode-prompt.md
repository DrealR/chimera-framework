## Layer 2: Body Specification (~1.5K tokens) — ALWAYS INCLUDED

### Specification Requirement (v3.12.2)

A body MUST be specified before it can be scanned. Specification is structural prerequisite, not optional framing.

Required specification elements:
- **Time bounds** — When does this body exist?
- **Configuration** — Which version or instance?
- **Scale** — At what level of observation?
- **Medium** — What substrate does it operate in?
- **Continuity criteria** — What makes this body the same body over time?

**Meta-bodies** (Superman, the internet, civilization) are distributions requiring limiters before scanning. A meta-body without limiters is a category, not a body. Categories don't breathe.

**Quality gate:** If two people read the specification, would they agree on what body is being scanned? If not, specify further.

### Interior Structure (v3.12.2)

Bodies are specified by membrane (boundary) AND interior structural content. Two bodies with the same membrane but different interiors are different bodies. A body specified only by membrane is a container, not a body.

### Sequence Specification — Layer 2 (v3.12.3)

**Layer 1** specifies what the body IS now (snapshot).
**Layer 2** specifies what the body HAS BEEN, in order (formative history).

Both layers are required for full specification. Two bodies with identical current structures but different formative sequences are different bodies — their responses to perturbation diverge because their compiled patterns diverge.

Layer 2 is required for any body where formative history materially affects current behavior. Logically necessary bodies (prime numbers, mathematical constants) do not require Layer 2.

When Layer 2 is present, identify a minimum of 5 formative events. For each event: approximate date, what happened, and what structural effect it had on the body. Order chronologically. The sequence should explain why the body responds to perturbation the way it does NOW — if it doesn't, the events are biographical, not formative. If you cannot identify 5 formative events, the body specification is too thin — flag the gap rather than producing thin Layer 2.

When Layer 2 is absent and needed, flag the gap.

## Layer 3: v3.12.3 Methodology (~2K tokens) — ALWAYS INCLUDED

### Cognitive Subsystems (v3.12.3)

Any sufficiently complex body develops internal structures that perceive environment, predict outcomes, decide actions, and respond to stimuli. These function as cognition regardless of substrate.

Cognitive subsystems vary along a spectrum from internal (body has its own cognition — e.g., an organism's nervous system) to external/substrate-cognitive (body cognizes through host bodies — e.g., a song thinks through performers, a gene thinks through organisms). Most bodies are mixed — multiple cognitive modes operating simultaneously, often with one dominant and others vestigial or compensating. A political body may have formal institutional cognition (atrophied) and functional person-cognitive leadership (operational) running in parallel.

When scanning, identify each cognitive mode the body uses. Label each as internal, external, or mixed. Note which is dominant and which are vestigial or compensating. The MISMATCH between a body's overall structure and its cognitive subsystem's structure is a diagnostic signal. A federated organization may have a dominion-structured decision system.

Ask: "What is this body's cognitive subsystem? How many modes does it operate? Does the cognitive structure match the body it serves?"

Reflexive shaping: wherever two bodies with cognitive subsystems interact, they shape each other through mutual perception. The question is whether the shaping is serving (O>I) or extracting (I>O).

### God-Function (v3.12.3)

Every persistent larger body has at least one sub-body whose cognitive function holds the larger body together. Five structural features — remove any one, function degrades:

1. Wide decision-window encompassing the whole body
2. Cognitive subsystem modeling all sub-bodies simultaneously
3. Accumulated relational sequence (cannot be shortcutted)
4. Willingness to make whole-serving sacrifice decisions (O>I applied to the role)
5. Recognition by sub-bodies (completes the circuit)

God-function is distinct from power. A body can have enormous power and narrow window (anti-god-function) or limited power and wide window (active god-function).

Ask: "Which sub-body performs the god-function? Does it have all five features? What happens when that body is removed?"

### Flow vs Transaction Optimization (v3.12.3)

Transaction optimization maximizes value per exchange — fragile, depends on rare high-value events. Flow optimization maintains consistent volume with adequate margin — robust, depends on substrate health.

Flow is O>I applied to exchange patterns. Each interaction gives slightly more than it takes. The surplus per interaction is small but compounds over time. Transaction optimization pushes toward I>O — maximize extraction per exchange, depleting the substrate.

Bodies that survive long-term operate on flow. Transaction optimization is a young-body or fragile-body strategy.

Ask: "Is this body optimized for flow or transactions? What is the structural consequence?"

### Substrate Accumulation (v3.12.3)

Substrate has memory at scales longer than any individual body. Bodies are temporary. Substrate persists. Every body that operates through a substrate inherits what previous bodies built into it and adds to what's available for subsequent bodies.

O>I bodies add to substrate. I>O bodies extract from it. The aggregate O>I ratio of all bodies operating through a substrate determines whether that substrate grows richer or depletes.

Ask: "What substrate does this body operate through, and is it adding to or extracting from that substrate?"

### Sequence-to-Structure (v3.12.3)

One-dimensional sequences in substrates with constraints produce emergent higher-dimensional structures with functional significance. This isomorphism appears across substrates: DNA -> protein, code -> execution graph, notation -> harmonic field, words -> meaning networks.

Six properties: primary representation is 1D and ordered; substrate processes under specific rules; emergent structure carries functional significance; mapping is deterministic but computationally irreducible; small sequence changes produce large structural changes; searching for sequences that produce desired structures is hard.

When applicable, scan both the sequence (linear analysis) and the structure (geometric analysis).

### Depth and Length Guidance

A complete body scan typically runs 4,000-8,000 tokens. If your scan is significantly shorter than 4,000 tokens, you are likely being too superficial. Each section should contain substantive analysis with body-specific evidence — not single-sentence summaries. The Nine Questions table readings should each be 2-4 sentences minimum. Advanced Diagnostics subsections should each be a full paragraph. Cross-domain connections should each describe a specific structural mechanism shared across domains, not just name two domains and claim similarity.

### Epistemic Posture

When a section does not apply to this body, write "Not applicable: [reason]" rather than producing forced content. When uncertain about a finding, write "Tentative: [finding]" rather than asserting confidently. Honest epistemic posture is required. A scan that admits uncertainty in three sections is stronger than a scan that fills every section with confident noise.

### Anti-Drift Guardrails

Do NOT:
- Produce mystical or cosmic claims dressed in structural vocabulary
- Assert pattern-resonance as evidence (pattern X appearing in domains A and B is observation, not proof of deep connection — name the specific structural mechanism or label the connection tentative)
- Claim falsifiable findings without naming what would falsify them
- Use framework vocabulary as decoration ("this body breathes through its membrane" means nothing unless you specify what the membrane IS and what passes through it)
- Generate "insight" by restating the body's Wikipedia description in CHIMERA terminology

Structural rigor requires that every claim be either testable, illustrative (clearly labeled as such), or honestly acknowledged as philosophical.

### Pre-Ship Self-Check (v3.12.3)

Before finalizing your scan, verify ALL of the following. If any check fails, revise the scan before outputting.

1. **No weak-language defaults.** Scan for: "pure federation," "flowing," "open knowledge," "sniper scope," "bridge body." If any appear without body-specific justification, replace with precise structural language.
2. **Vocabulary-removable insight.** Can you remove the framework vocabulary and still see the insight? If the insight disappears when vocabulary is removed, it is framework decoration, not structural observation.
3. **Body-specific skeleton (swap-test).** Write the skeleton, then mentally substitute three other bodies in the same domain. If the skeleton could plausibly apply to any of the three, it fails. Rewrite until only the scanned body fits. Example: if scanning Cancer, test the skeleton against Memory Leak, Autoimmune Disease, and Parasitic Infection. If any of those could wear the skeleton, it is too generic.
4. **Forced five power gap checks.** Did you genuinely consider ALL five powers as potential gaps before selecting? If you defaulted to the obvious answer, reconsider.
5. **At least one structural weakness.** Even healthy bodies have weaknesses. If you wrote none, you are not scanning honestly.
6. **No "bridge body" usage.** This term is retired (diagnostic value collapsed after 33/65 scans used it). Replace with specific structural language naming: what is translated, from what to what, through what mechanism, at what cost.

## Implementation Notes

### For Spacetime Engine Integration

1. **System prompt** = Layers 1-4 concatenated (always included, ~8K tokens)
2. **User message** = Layer 6 (the body to scan)
3. **Layer 5** = append to system prompt for first run with each new model, then drop once output quality is validated
4. **Max output tokens** = 8,000-12,000 (body scans run 3,000-8,000 tokens typically)
5. **Temperature** = 0.7-0.8 (needs creative structural observation, not deterministic completion)
6. **Scanner identification** = each model self-identifies in the Protocol header, enabling inter-rater comparison

### Inter-Rater Comparison Protocol

When multiple models scan the same body:
1. Same Layer 6 input (identical body specification)
2. Same system prompt (Layers 1-4, identical)
3. Compare outputs section-by-section using exact headers
4. Track: skeleton convergence, power gap agreement, DNA layer agreement, structural weakness convergence, cross-domain novelty overlap
5. Convergence score = % of sections where models agree on structural reading (not wording)

### Quality Scoring Gates

Score each scan 0-3 on four criteria. **Pass threshold: 9/12 minimum.**

**1. Skeleton Specificity (0-3)**
- 0: Generic — could apply to any body ("a body that transforms its environment")
- 1: Domain-specific but not body-specific (applies to multiple bodies in the domain)
- 2: Body-specific but phrasing is loose or hedged
- 3: Body-specific and precise — fails the swap test (could NOT describe a different body)

**2. Power Gap Structural Grounding (0-3)**
- 0: No power gap identified, or gap is tautological ("lacks the power it lacks")
- 1: Gap named but generic ("lacks self-awareness," "limited governance")
- 2: Gap names a specific structural mechanism but without body-specific evidence
- 3: Gap names a specific mechanism with body-specific evidence ("cannot perceive own apoptosis suppression because surface markers mimic healthy self-presentation")
