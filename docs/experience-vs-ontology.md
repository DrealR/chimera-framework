# Experience vs Ontology

A small, sharp rule for the drawer.

**An experience cannot certify its own ontology when the verifying instrument is the thing being modified.** Psychedelics, trance, deep meditation, fever, any altered state: the evaluator is *inside the event.* The instrument reading "this is more real than reality" is the same instrument the state is currently rewriting. A scale that gets heavier when it weighs heavy things cannot be used to prove things are heavy.

It cuts both ways, and the symmetry is the whole point:

- The altered state **cannot prove the realm.** The vividness, the certainty, the sense of contact with something fundamental — all are *exactly* what a modified instrument would report, so they're not evidence of an external realm. They're evidence the instrument was modified.
- **Sober materialism cannot disprove it either.** The hard problem of consciousness is open; "it's just neurons" is a claim with its own unpaid bills. The default state is not a neutral, ontology-revealing instrument either.

## The administrable test

Judge altered states by **the writes they leave in behavior** — the only test you can actually run ([behaviors are writes](behaviors-are-writes.md)). Did the experience change how the person acts, treats people, works, shows up? That's measurable, it's the same criterion as the Egyptian judgment scene, and it doesn't require settling what the experience *was*.

So: realm claims go in the [drawer](failure-mode-specimen-drawer.md) — resonant, unsigned, not promoted, not denied. The **writes stay on the books as real**, because behavior change is observable regardless of its cause. You lose nothing true by refusing to sign the ontology, and you keep everything that actually transferred.
