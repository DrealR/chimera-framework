# The Walk/Teleport Criteria

A decision tool. For any stretch of any path — a task, a skill, a build step, a commute, a problem — one master question: **does the value live in the destination or in the path?**

Destination only: teleport. Delegate it, automate it, look it up, hand it to the machine, without guilt. Path: walk it, because the walk is the product.

## The five tests

Run them per stretch, not per project. Any strong YES is a vote for walking.

1. **RESIDUE** — will traversing this change my weights? If the walk writes something into me that I keep (skill, intuition, calibration), the walk has value independent of the destination.
2. **RECURRENCE** — will I meet this road again? Recurring roads compound: walking them once buys every future traversal. One-off roads don't.
3. **OPENNESS** — does the destination exist before I walk? Closed problems have pre-existing answers; teleport freely. Open problems *must* be walked, because the walk is the authorship — there is no destination until you author one. Laundering an open problem as closed is the core identity failure ([identity as residue](identity-as-residue-of-walked-paths.md)).
4. **EVALUATOR** — can I judge the teleported output? Execution delegates; **evaluation never does.** The minimum walk in any domain you depend on is whatever keeps the evaluator alive. The day you can't read what the machine ships is the day the work stops being yours, regardless of who wrote the seed.
5. **RELATIONSHIP** — does anyone's trust form on this path? Bonds are written in shared traversal. A road walked together is doing two jobs; teleporting it silently cancels the second one.

## The two guards

**The boundary is indexed to the walker, not the road.** There is no universal list of "things you should never automate." Mastery is an *expanding license to teleport, earned by walking.* The expert teleports stretches the learner must walk — legitimately, because the expert's evaluator is alive there and the learner's isn't yet. EARN YOUR TELEPORTS. Learners walk first, teleport second.

**Friction is not sacred.** Suffering through dead transport is waste, not virtue. The discipline this tool teaches is *discrimination*, not asceticism. Walking a closed, non-recurring, no-residue road on principle is just slow.

## The consequence

The human/AI ratio is an **output** of these tests, never an input. "How much AI should I use?" has no answer in the abstract; run the five tests on each stretch and the ratio falls out. Anyone selling a fixed ratio — all-in or abstinence — is answering before measuring.

## The kid version

Four questions, one per pocket: *Will this road make me stronger? Will I see this road again? Does the answer exist before I start? Could I catch the machine being wrong?* Any yes: hands on the wheel for that stretch.

## Known open edge

The evaluator test has a bootstrap problem: you cannot evaluate your own evaluator from inside it. The patch is external calibration — reality (does the build stand), other walkers (do they catch what you missed), real load (does the kid actually use it). This is the deepest function of the relationship criterion, and it is why shipping ([the seed protocol](the-seed-protocol.md), move four) is part of the epistemics, not just the delivery. See also [meet AI where you are](meet-ai-where-you-are.md) for the altitude version of the evaluator rule.

## The scene version

The five tests govern *work* stretches. [Scenes and the witness](scenes-and-the-witness.md) gives the social companion — four checks on entering any scene: who are the observers and are they real, is there a shared referee or is the scene rendered per-viewer, am I actor or spectator here (only actors get written), and which mode does this scene need (cooled service or the operator). "Control your scene" made operational.
