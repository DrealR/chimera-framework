# Two Baselines of Trust — Come-to-You and Holds-Its-Own

> **Origin:** Walked the cat/dog distinction until the wording pinned (June 2026). A sharper tool than
> "meet them where they are": *read which trust-baseline* someone runs, and love them the way that
> fits. Reemy's cat and squirrels taught it. Firewalls applied in writing.
>
> **Date:** June 2026

---

## Two baselines

There are two different baseline postures toward trust and space, and loving each teaches a different
kind of love:

- **Comes-to-you** (the dog). Open-trust: it offers trust outward, comes willingly, organizes around
  your leadership — you're the operator and it follows. It teaches the **love that receives trust and
  leads well:** something comes to you openly, and you honor that openness by being a good operator
  for it.
- **Holds-its-own** (the cat). Sovereign-trust: it's its own little operator, holds its own space, and
  comes *on its own terms* once you've made the space safe — you don't direct it, you set conditions
  and let it choose. It teaches the **love that respects sovereignty and waits:** something holds its
  own, and you honor that by setting good conditions and waiting for it to choose you.

Both are *full* trust. The difference is posture, not amount.

---

## You can't force a cat to be a dog

Try to make a holds-its-own be a comes-to-you — demand it come, direct it, expect open-trust from it —
and you **break it,** because that's not how it operates. So the skill is to **recognize which
baseline you're dealing with and love it accordingly:** lead the one that comes to you, make space for
the one that holds its own.

This maps onto people exactly, and that's where it's useful. Some people are **comes-to-you**
(they arrive open, they trust, they organize around a leader); some are **holds-its-own** (they keep
their own space, they come on their own terms, they need conditions set and *not* forced). The hurt,
standoffish person is holds-its-own: they don't need you to force connection — they need you to set a
safe space, prove consistency over time, and let them come. This is [meeting people where their
attention is](people-are-co-operators-not-instruments.md), with a sharper read: *which baseline,* then
the love that fits.

And the squirrels prove it's not about the type: in the *same* species, some come fast (open-trust)
and some hold back (sovereign-trust, still part of the body but on their own terms). It's the
**trust-posture, not the animal.**

## Firewall

- **Drop positive/negative.** The cat is **not** the negative version of the dog — that smuggles a
  false hierarchy (less-good, less-loving) and it's exactly what tangles the wording. Use
  **comes-to-you / holds-its-own** (or open-trust / sovereign-trust). The holds-its-own is the
  *sovereign* version of trust, equally full, just earned differently.
- **No damage-hierarchy.** The holds-its-own baseline is usually **earned wisdom** — an appropriate
  response to having been hurt, or to surviving a space where trust wasn't safe. It's a **sovereignty
  to honor, not a woundedness to cure.** Keep the compassion (people go cat-like for real reasons);
  drawer "open-trust is pure, sovereign-trust is damaged." The wariness is wisdom.
- **The resolution is the tree-move.** You don't force trust; you can only be **reliably safe and let
  it be chosen.** Be the consistent space, set the conditions, wait — and the sovereign ones organize
  around it in their own time (see [the consistent force](the-leverage-is-inward.md)).
