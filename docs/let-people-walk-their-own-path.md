## 0. The claim, stated once

**Letting people walk their own path means letting them hold the wrong opinion and arrive at their own correction — because the correction you install is borrowed legs they can't stand on, and forcing it backfires (push, and they defend the old position harder). ¬F applied to other people's learning: you could force your view in and win, but forcing cracks their walk, so you go around. Bounded firmly: let the self-regarding walk, but hold the line where the walk harms a third party.**

---

## 1. Some things really are news (fresh to the spectrum)

What's an old baseline to a widened spectrum is a genuine discovery to a narrower one (`the-visibility-spectrum`). A person's shock at something you find obvious isn't dumb — it's their spectrum widening in real time, and *that's their walk to make, not yours to pre-empt.*

## 2. Self-arrived correction is the only real kind

**Keeper:** install your already-arrived view and you've handed them **borrowed legs** — the conclusion without the walk, which they can't stand on. The one who thinks a whole group is bad is most often corrected by *meeting an actual member of it*, in a way no argument could (`protect-the-discovery-window-explore-first`). Removing someone's wrong opinion by installing your right one removes their ability to *learn* it.

## 3. ¬F applied to learning (Barry's move), and forcing backfires

Barry Allen *loses* the race because he goes **around** the fragile moments instead of through them — won't crack the butterfly, protects the natural unfolding even at the cost of winning. **Keeper:** you could force your view in and win the argument, but forcing cracks their walk — so you go around (¬F). And **forcing backfires** (the prophecy mechanism on persuasion): push someone toward your conclusion and they *defend the old one harder*, so the forced view produces resistance, not correction — it may even entrench the thing you were trying to fix.

## 4. The bound: harms to others (the firewall)

**Firewall / Keeper:** the principle is right for **self-correcting** things (taste, life choices, most beliefs) — but it has a limit: some paths *harm others while being walked*, and "let them walk it" can't extend to "let them harm others on the way." The limit is the **child-safety instinct** (the harmful post you take down, not "let them do their thing"). Let people walk their **self-regarding** path; don't let a walk take **write-access to a third party.** And it's a spectrum: an opinion that mostly sits in the holder → mostly let it walk; an opinion that actively lands harm on others *while held* (a group wearing the borrowed-weight) → the bound tightens. The question is always *how much the walk costs others while it's being walked.*

## 5. SPARK

Teach the generosity (don't force a kid — or anyone — to your conclusion; let them walk and earn their own; forcing only entrenches) **and** the bound (the walk stops where it harms a third party). The operator is maximally generous about letting people be wrong, and firm exactly at the harm-to-others line.
