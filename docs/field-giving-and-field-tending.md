# Field-Giving and Field-Tending

> **Classification:** contribution practice and public-field design principle. This extends positioned planting beyond publication strategy by distinguishing a direct gift from a contribution made available to a shared environment.

## The claim

> **Field-giving enriches a shared medium without assigning the receiver, requiring acknowledgment, or owning the translation.**

A direct gift says, “this is for you.” A field gift says, “this is now available in the world we temporarily share.” Music in a park, public gardening, open-source software, a cared-for common room, and freely offered knowledge can all take this form.

```text
source body creates
→ shared medium changes
→ multiple bodies encounter the change differently
→ each may approach, ignore, transform, or leave
→ the giver reads the field and releases ownership
```

The gift is real even when its reception is unrecorded. A contribution need not become a metric, relationship, or permanent artifact to have altered a lived moment.

## Five conditions

Field-giving remains generative when it preserves:

1. **Coherent source** — the contribution comes from a real practice or care, not an empty attention lure.
2. **Shared medium** — it enters a place, codebase, commons, room, or conversation others actually inhabit.
3. **Optional reception** — bodies may receive, decline, reinterpret, or leave.
4. **Calibration** — volume, duration, placement, and cost remain responsive to the local body.
5. **Non-pursuit** — the contributor does not chase each receiver for proof that the gift worked.

> **A gift remains a gift when people are free not to receive it.**

## Field-tending

Non-pursuit is not indifference. A living contributor reads the return without converting it into a demand.

```text
perceive the field
→ contribute
→ notice embodied return
→ modulate volume, timing, or form
→ release
```

This is **field-tending**: participation with feedback and restraint. It differs from broadcasting because the environment can change the next expression. It differs from capture because the receiver retains veto and exit.

## Social membrane

A field gift can create graded contact between isolation and direct relationship:

```text
ambient availability
→ passing recognition
→ mutual awareness
→ nonverbal response
→ optional conversation
→ possible recurring relation
```

The membrane lets bodies choose distance. A listener can receive a few seconds, a user can fork the code, a passerby can continue walking, and a participant can approach without being made responsible for the giver's aliveness.

## Expression without pursuit

Field-giving separates two operations often collapsed online:

- **expression:** placing a coherent signal into a shared medium;
- **pursuit:** attempting to secure a particular receiver, response, metric, or identity outcome.

Expression can invite relation. Pursuit begins to force it when the giver cannot tolerate independent reception.

## Shadow: field capture

Field-giving inverts when:

- volume removes the practical ability not to receive;
- the environment becomes a stage whose inhabitants were not consulted;
- optional response becomes social obligation;
- the signal exists mainly to harvest attention;
- every return is managed until the field can no longer answer independently.

The public character of a medium does not erase local membranes. Access to a field is not ownership of it.

## Field-giving scan

1. What shared body am I entering?
2. What am I adding that was not available before?
3. Can nearby bodies decline or leave without penalty?
4. What costs—noise, space, moderation, maintenance, attention—land on others?
5. What feedback should change the next pass?
6. Can I release the contribution without demanding proof of reception?
7. Does the field become more habitable and more capable of independent creation?
