## 0. The claim, stated once

**Deep domain knowledge is the freedom to move freely within the domain — to flow, recombine the building blocks, change direction at will. It's the energy-cost-of-crossing inverted: mastery makes redirection cheap in that domain. And it resolves the AI-divide: AI amplifies freedom-of-movement for those who have it (superpowers) and substitutes for it for those who don't (a crutch that keeps them stuck). ## 1. Mastery = freedom of movement (energy-cost inverted)

## 2. The AI-divide, resolved

**Keeper:** AI **amplifies** freedom-of-movement for the master (accelerate further, more directions to fly) and **substitutes** for it for the novice (an easy-way-out that keeps them where they are). Same tool, opposite effect, by domain-depth — which is why the experts seem to contradict each other and people suspect they're lying: they're not; everyone's report is true *from their position* (the master: "AI 10x'd me"; the novice: "AI let me ship without understanding"). The instrument-is-dead-until-a-live-hand-plays-it, at career scale.

## 3. Bridge the gap actively — the false-confidence trap

**Keeper:** the default of relying on AI **keeps you in your current realm** (start from the AI-side → the gap stays). Passive reliance is the deepening groove that keeps you stuck; active bridging is *paying the energy cost to cross into mastery while you can.* The trap: **false confidence** — lying to yourself that you understand (when AI did it) installs a *hollow groove*, and false confidence compounds (rabbit-holes, building on what you don't stand on; `knowing-as-installation` — borrowed legs on your own skill). The defense: **brutal honesty about what you actually understand** — never ship a line (or claim a skill) you can't explain.
