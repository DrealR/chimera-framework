# The Seed Protocol

A build protocol for the AI era. It is the month's findings put in build order, and it answers the question that fires every time a machine hands you a finished thing: **is this mine?**

## The mechanical answer to "is it yours"

AI building is a teleportation event ([the two roots](the-two-roots.md)): the path is walked inside the machine, in a frame you can't experience, and the deliverable arrives fully formed. Identity survives a transformation only when there's a **continuity carrier** — the thing tracked through time while the parts get swapped. So:

**The build is yours if and only if you supplied the continuity carrier. The carrier is the hand-walked seed.**

The seed is the one page you wrote yourself: the chosen center, the constraints, the refusals, the taste decisions, the thing the whole build grows from and gets judged against. That's the plank that never gets replaced. When the seed is yours and the [evaluator](walk-teleport-criteria.md) is yours, the body that arrives is yours no matter how much of the middle teleported — the same way you're still you despite your cells turning over, because the pattern tracked through time was never the cells.

When there's no seed — you opened the editor and said "build me something good" — there's no carrier, and what arrives is a **median body wearing your name.** Hollow, in the exact way bad builds always felt hollow before there was a word for it.

## The four moves

1. **Split the path.** Before anything, mark which stretches are *open* and which are *closed*. Open: the destination doesn't exist until you author it — what this is for, who it's for, what it should feel like, what it refuses. Closed: there's a pre-existing right answer — boilerplate, syntax, scaffolding, config. This is the [openness test](walk-teleport-criteria.md) run on the build plan itself.

2. **Walk the open stretches by hand, first**, before the machine touches anything. This is writing the seed. It's usually small — an hour against the machine's minutes — but it's the identity-bearing stretch, where the residue lands, the carrier for everything after.

3. **Teleport the closed stretches hard, no guilt** — that's the license you earned by walking. But run the evaluator on everything that comes back, which requires staying walked-enough in every domain you depend on to catch the machine being wrong. *The day you can't read what the machine ships is the day the build stops being yours, regardless of who wrote the seed.*

4. **Cool it.** Ship it; put it under real load — a real user, a real kid, the park. (Move 3 of the [3-move mechanism](write-access-to-your-own-attention.md): build something load-bearing so it cools.) A build that never ships never cools, never becomes load-bearing, and stays a beautiful document — which is [design-loop substitution](key-principles.md) wearing a hard hat.

## The aliveness test

For any finished body, three checks. A body is alive when it has:

- **A chosen center** — the one thing it orbits, *appointed, not found.* The local sun.
- **A seam** — the trade-off it openly negotiates instead of pretending away. Every real instrument has its [G-to-B](guitar-proofs-ledger.md): the one asymmetry everything else bends around.
- **Walked weight at the open joints** — the places where decisions had no pre-existing answer carry a particular walker's residue. You can feel a path in them.

Hollow bodies fail all three the same way: no center because nothing was refused, no seam because no trade-off was faced, median everywhere because every open question got laundered as closed ([identity as residue](identity-as-residue-of-walked-paths.md)).

## It ports past building

- **Guitar:** a song's *form* is mostly closed — the chords of Stand By Me pre-exist; learn them, that's licensed teleportation through a solved space. The *performance* is open: the breath, the timing in the park with real faces. No destination until you walk it; can't be delegated. (It's why the park felt different from the bedroom — open stretch, walked live.)
- **Comedy:** joke *structure* is teleportable — setup/punchline mechanics are closed and learnable from anyone. The *observation* must come from your walked path, which is mechanically why stolen jokes feel hollow even when delivered well. The audience runs the residue test in real time; funny is partly an aliveness detector.

## The one concrete move

Next build: write the seed by hand before opening anything. One page — center, refusals, feel, seam. Time it. Then count what fraction of the whole build that hour was versus the teleported rest, and check the output against the aliveness test. If the seeded build feels different in your hands than the unseeded ones, the protocol has weight. If not, revise it. The proof is never the map; it's the build.
