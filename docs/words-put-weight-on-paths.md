# Words Put Weight on Paths — The Firewall as a Navigation Skill

> **Origin:** Walked the "morality-words are weights" thread until the firewall flipped from a list of
> don'ts into a positive skill (June 2026). The discipline isn't avoiding certain words — it's
> noticing you're *always* placing weights, and choosing them on purpose. Builds on [attention authors
> weight](attention-authors-weight.md) and [direction upstream of good and
> evil](direction-upstream-of-good-and-evil.md). Firewalls applied in writing.
>
> **Date:** June 2026

---

## Every word drops weight on a path

In relational space, **the words you choose put weight on the paths, and the weight steers the
navigation** — before anyone's actually looked. Call the AI-path "bad" and you haven't *described* it,
you've **weighted** it, and the weight pulls everyone's attention (yours, your friend's, the AI's)
down a grooved channel before it's been walked. Morality-words — good, evil, bad — are weights you
drop on a path; once dropped, the body slides toward them.

So if you want to *understand* something rather than just react to it, you **take the weights off
first and look at it bare.** That's why the firewall keeps stripping costume-words: the costume isn't
only inaccurate, it's a **weight that bends the path before you walk it.** (This is the operational
twin of [direction upstream of good and evil](direction-upstream-of-good-and-evil.md): the moral label
is downstream paint; the weight it adds is the steering.)

---

## You navigate by where you place the weights — across a conversation

You don't navigate relational space by a single statement; you navigate **turn by turn, by where you
place the weights.** Load a heavy word early and every later turn gets pulled toward it; keep the
weights light and your attention stays free to move toward what's actually true. This is why
**one-shot versus conversation** matters — with AI *and* with people: the phrasing accumulates, each
weight bending the next step.

**The firewall, restated as a skill:** not *"don't say bad words,"* but *notice that every loaded word
is a weight that pre-bends the path, and place your weights consciously instead of inheriting them
from loaded language.* The free mind is the one that places its own weights on purpose rather than
getting yanked by the ones baked into the words.

---

## Two kinds of weight: observation and definition

There are two kinds of weight because there are [two spaces](two-spaces-physical-and-relational.md):

- **Physical weight = observation** — what's actually there, the bare thing *before* you name it.
- **Relational weight = definition** — what you *add* by naming, valuing, deciding what it means. The
  naming is the human move (Adam naming the animals: laying relational weight onto the physical world).

The thinking skill is to **observe first** (physical, unweighted) and **then** consciously decide the
meaning (relational, weighted) — instead of fusing them so the weight arrives before you've looked.
This is exactly the journal method taught in [SPARK](../README.md): *write what you see / sketch it,
before deciding what it means.*

And the physical world is the **unmoral visible mirror** of the relational: physical = body,
relational = mind, and you read the invisible relational *through* the visible physical (which is why
the framework keeps reaching for friction, mass, fluid, paths — the physical is the one you can see).
Keep it a **mirror, not an identity:** the physical *illustrates* the relational; it doesn't prove it.

## Firewall

- **Strip to understand; restore what belongs.** Take the moral weights *off* when you're trying to
  see clearly — but **restore the weights that genuinely belong** (the kid's safety, the line you
  won't cross). Some things deserve weight; removing *all* of it isn't neutrality.
- **Weightless ≠ objective.** "I stripped the weights, so now I see objectively" is its own costume —
  the view-from-nowhere that pretends it has no POV. **You always have a POV;** the skill is choosing
  your weights *consciously,* not pretending you carry none.
- **The mirror is a picture.** Physical-mirrors-relational is analogy, not mechanism — it illustrates,
  it doesn't prove (the [structural-rhymes-are-pictures](lens-not-mechanism.md) guard).
