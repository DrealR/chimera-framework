## 0. The claim, stated once

**Nobody loves a topic; the love is a *way of engaging* — understanding something deeply enough to see the beauty in it. So you can't force-yourself-to-love an obligation (money-pressure is anti-fusion; it pulls the task from the love-zone to the obligation-zone). Instead, don't ask "can I love this task?" — ask "what do I love that has the same skeleton as this task?" and build that. The skeleton transfers the skill; the love stays clean.**

---

## 1. The love is in the *how*, not the *what*

**Keeper:** the love isn't the subject — it's the depth-of-engagement (going deep enough to see the beauty and connect it to what you care about). So *any* obligation *could* be loved by going deep — **but** forcing the money-task as the thing-you-love often *kills* the fun, because money-pressure is anti-fusion. The resolution is not "force the love" — it's **decouple the skill-building-you-love from the obligation-prep.**

## 2. Strip-to-skeleton on your own learning

**Keeper:** strip the obligation to its *shape* (e.g. a records system = structured-data + rules + lifecycle + retrieval), then find that shape in your own world (a craft, a domain, a passion) and build the **loved version.** Same bone, different meat — the meat you love. The skill transfers because the skeleton is identical; the love survives because you're building the thing you'd build anyway. `strip-to-the-skeleton` applied to your own learning.

## 3. The love-project de-risks the obligation

**Keeper:** building the loved version first means the obligation, when it arrives, is **"same shape — I know this,"** not scary first-contact. The love-project keeps the judgment sharp, builds the transferable skill, and protects the love from the money-pressure that would have contaminated it. *(Fusion, not obligation: find the loved thing that teaches the work.)*

## 4. Find-your-own-way

**Keeper:** this pairs with *find your own way of developing* — don't copy other people's workflows; the mode that fits you (for many, conversational design-through-back-and-forth) is a genuine working style, and it matches where the field is heading (value moved to design/judgment/steering). The goal isn't "force myself through the obligation for money" — it's *find the way in that makes it deep and fun*, the way a good lens makes anything beautiful. The money follows the love, not the other way around.
