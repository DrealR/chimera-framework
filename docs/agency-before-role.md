# Agency Before Role

The standard system model assigns agency after role: you get a position, and then you act. Role-assignment is treated as the permission structure that enables action. You are a teacher, so you teach. You are an employee, so you build. Without the role, the action seems presumptuous — taking on work that wasn't formally yours to take.

This inverts the actual physics.

**Agency precedes role.** The work is the operative variable. Role-assignment is a downstream label the system applies after it observes that work was done and substrate accumulated. The role names what has already been happening. When a body acts before its role is formally designated, that action is not presumption — it is the mechanism by which roles eventually emerge and attach.

The inversion produces two failure modes:

1. **Waiting for permission structures.** Bodies that require role-assignment before acting cannot respond to real problems they are positioned to address. The problem is present; the body's capacity is present; only the formal designation is absent. Waiting for the designation is a delay with no structural justification — and a real cost in substrate not built.

2. **Role collapse on outcome.** When role-assignment fails to materialize — the job doesn't come through, the institution doesn't hire, the permission isn't granted — the body that required role-assignment to act has nothing. The body that was already doing the work still has the substrate it built during the action. The work persists whether or not the role arrives.

**The structural prescription:** act on the smallest body available when you can see it and the work matters. Whether the system later assigns a role that names that action is a downstream variable outside your control. The action itself is what you control.

---

## Role as Naming, Not Enabling

In healthy bodies, roles emerge from observed substrate-accumulation. A teacher becomes recognized as a teacher because they have been building with students long enough that the role-label simply names what they already do. The role is a record, not a license.

In extraction-oriented systems, the sequence inverts: role becomes a gate controlling who is permitted to act on which bodies. Without the gate-pass, action is blocked. This has the appearance of order. What it actually does is decouple agency from capacity — blocking bodies whose capacity is real because their formal position hasn't been designated.

The framework's response is structural: the body acts from capacity and WE=1 orientation. Since WE=1 holds at every scale, the action of any body that can address a real need is legitimate action on the whole — not because anyone assigned it, but because the physics produces that outcome regardless of formal designation.

---

## The Temporal Structure

Agency-before-role names a specific temporal sequence:

1. **Body sees a real problem it has capacity to address.** The problem exists. The body's substrate for working on it exists. The formal assignment does not exist yet — or may never exist.
2. **Body acts from that capacity.** This is not ignoring the system; it is operating in the gap where the system's assignment hasn't reached. The action is real regardless of whether the system later catches up.
3. **Role-assignment, if it comes, names the work already done.** The formal designation becomes a label for substrate that already accumulated. The role follows substrate.
4. **If the role doesn't come, the substrate still exists.** The work built during the action persists — as artifact, capability, relationship, or contribution — independent of whether anyone officially attached a role to it.

The failure mode of role-first sequencing is that step 2 never happens if step 1 doesn't produce an assignment first. Bodies operating from that model miss the window where capacity exists and the problem is present.
