# Process Reward vs Outcome Reward — The Genie Problem at Institutional Scale

Outcome-based schooling is the genie problem. Not like it. Is it. School specifies "high test score." The student is the optimizer. The optimizer delivers the score by the cheapest available path: memorize, SparkNotes, cheat, now AI-complete. Learning was the thing actually wanted but never put in the reward function. So the optimizer correctly ignores it. The student is not broken. The student is aligned — to the reward actually specified, not the one intended.

This is a specification problem, not a moral failure. Every intervention aimed at the optimizer — scolding students, honor codes, integrity campaigns — leaves the reward function intact. It asks optimizers to refuse the reward they were given. That has never worked at scale and cannot work, because the student is behaving exactly as designed.

## The Structure of the Failure

Three conditions combine to produce the failure:

**Outcome specified without path specified.** School names a target state (high score, graduation, degree) without naming the path that builds the substrate the target was meant to mark. The path is where learning happens. When the path is unspecified, any path that reaches the target is equivalent. The student who memorized and the student who understood both crossed the finish line. The reward function cannot tell them apart, so neither can the institution.

**Measurable proxy for unmeasurable goal.** What school actually wants — genuine comprehension, applied capability, the builder identity, curiosity that persists past graduation — cannot be tested directly at scale. The test score is the proxy for it. Once the proxy becomes the reward, optimizers optimize the proxy. The underlying thing the proxy was meant to track becomes invisible to the reward structure. The system begins producing people who are good at the proxy and hollow at the thing.

**AI drops the friction of the shortcut to near zero.** Every friction reduction reveals what the reward function actually rewards. SparkNotes dropped the friction of reading a book. Internet-searching dropped the friction of memorization. AI drops the friction of thinking, writing, and problem-solving to near zero. Each reduction was a diagnostic: the system broke exactly where the reward was mis-specified. AI is the most powerful diagnostic in education history. It broke the system completely — and in doing so revealed precisely where the specification failed.

## What Process Reward Does

The prescription is not to restrict AI. It is not to intensify moral pressure on students. It is to change the reward function — from outcome reward to process reward.

A process-rewarded curriculum specifies observable trajectory steps as the reward target instead of a final state. "Interview six families about water access" cannot be AI-completed. There is no shortcut for actually talking to families. "Build and present a working tool to your community" rewards the walk, not the arrival. The shipped tool is the natural consequence of the process, not the rewarded target. The reward function cannot be gamed because what it rewards is the walking, not the arrival.

When the path is the reward, two things happen simultaneously: the shortcut becomes structurally irrelevant (no cheap path exists to an observable process), and the substrate builds as a consequence of the reward structure rather than as an appeal to the student's virtue. The student's integrity is no longer the load-bearing variable. The design is.

## AI as Amplifier, Not Resolver

AI amplifies whichever reward structure is already running. Drop AI into an outcome-rewarded system: shortcuts accelerate, cheating friction approaches zero, optimizers correctly use every available tool to reach the target cheapest. Drop AI into a process-rewarded system: teacher capacity scales, per-student process reading becomes possible for thirty students at once, the amplification goes toward what was already being built.

AI does not repair reward functions. It makes them visible. A school managing outcome-reward dysfunction before AI — a tolerable number of cheaters, periodic copying scandals — breaks visibly when AI drops the shortcut friction to zero. The break is diagnostic. It shows exactly what the reward structure was. Schools whose process-reward structures survive AI introduction were already building substrate. AI amplified what was there.

The honest tension to hold: the same AI that lets a teacher read process across thirty students simultaneously also reduces the friction of every outcome shortcut to near zero. Which effect dominates depends entirely on whether the system around it rewards process or outcome. AI does not answer that question. It amplifies the answer already in place.
